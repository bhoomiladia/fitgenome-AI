# Recovery and Sleep for Training Performance

## Sleep and Muscle Recovery

### Why Sleep Matters for Fitness
Sleep is the primary recovery window for the body. During deep sleep (NREM Stage 3), the body releases growth hormone (GH), repairs damaged muscle tissue, and consolidates motor learning from training sessions.

### Sleep Duration Guidelines
| Sleep Duration | Impact on Training | Programming Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| < 5 hours | Severely impaired: -20-30% strength, poor recovery | Skip training or light active recovery only |
| 5-6 hours | Impaired: -10-15% performance, increased injury risk | Reduce volume by 20%, add extra rest day |
| 6-7 hours | Suboptimal: slight performance reduction | Maintain current program, prioritize sleep hygiene |
| 7-8 hours | Optimal for most adults | Full training capacity, progressive overload on track |
| 8-9 hours | Ideal for high-volume training or athletes | Maximum recovery, can handle higher training loads |

### Sleep Quality Factors
- **Consistent sleep schedule**: Same bed/wake times (±30 min) — most important factor
- **Sleep environment**: Cool (18-20°C), dark, quiet
- **Pre-sleep nutrition**: Casein protein or curd 30 min before bed supports overnight MPS
- **Caffeine cutoff**: No caffeine after 2 PM (half-life = 5-6 hours)
- **Screen reduction**: Minimize blue light 1 hour before bed
- **Magnesium**: 200-400mg before bed may improve sleep quality

## Active Recovery

### Low-Intensity Recovery Activities
- Walking: 20-30 minutes at conversational pace
- Yoga/stretching: 15-20 minutes focused on trained muscle groups
- Swimming: light laps for 15-20 minutes (minimal joint stress)
- Foam rolling: 5-10 minutes targeting major muscle groups

### Recovery Metrics to Track
- **Resting heart rate (RHR)**: Elevated RHR (+5-10 bpm above baseline) suggests incomplete recovery
- **Heart rate variability (HRV)**: Lower HRV indicates higher stress/poorer recovery
- **Subjective readiness**: Rate morning energy 1-10 scale; below 6 = potential overtraining
- **Sleep quality score**: Track hours + wake-ups + morning restfulness

## Stress and Cortisol Management

### Impact on Training
Chronic elevated cortisol (from stress, poor sleep, overtraining) leads to:
- Increased muscle protein breakdown
- Fat storage, especially visceral (belly) fat
- Weakened immune function
- Poor workout performance and motivation

### Mitigation Strategies
- **Training volume autoregulation**: Use RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) instead of fixed loads during high-stress periods
- **Deload weeks**: Every 4-6 weeks, reduce training volume by 40-50% while maintaining intensity
- **Stress-reducing practices**: 10 min meditation, breathwork (box breathing: 4-4-4-4)
- **Social recovery**: Training with a partner improves adherence and mood
- **Nature exposure**: Outdoor walks reduce cortisol by 15-20% (validated by research)

## Hydration for Recovery

### Water Requirements
- Baseline: 35-40 ml per kg bodyweight daily
- Training days: add 500-750 ml per hour of exercise
- Hot climate (Indian summers): add 500-1000 ml extra

### Electrolyte Balance
- Sodium: 1.5-3g/day (Indian diets typically sufficient)
- Potassium: bananas, coconut water, sweet potatoes
- Magnesium: nuts, seeds, dark leafy greens (palak)
- Signs of dehydration: dark urine, headache, fatigue, muscle cramps

## Recovery Nutrition

### Post-Workout Window (0-2 hours)
- Protein: 20-40g high-quality protein (whey, eggs, paneer)
- Carbs: 0.5-1g per kg bodyweight (rice, banana, roti)
- Ratio: 1:1 to 1:3 protein:carb depending on session intensity

### Anti-Inflammatory Foods (Indian Kitchen)
- Turmeric (haldi): curcumin reduces DOMS and inflammation
- Ginger (adrak): anti-inflammatory, aids digestion
- Garlic (lehsun): antioxidant, immune support
- Cinnamon (dalchini): blood sugar regulation
- Amla (Indian gooseberry): highest natural vitamin C source

## Weekly Recovery Structure

### Sample Recovery-Integrated Training Week
| Day | Training | Recovery Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Heavy compound lifts | Post-workout protein + carbs |
| Tuesday | Upper body hypertrophy | 8+ hours sleep target |
| Wednesday | Active recovery / light cardio | Yoga, foam rolling, meditation |
| Thursday | Lower body hypertrophy | Extra hydration, massage |
| Friday | Push/pull training | Pre-sleep casein/curd |
| Saturday | Light cardio or sport | Social activity, outdoor walk |
| Sunday | Complete rest | Sleep in, meal prep, stretching |
