# Progressive Overload Principles

## Definition
Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed on the musculoskeletal system during resistance training. It is the single most important principle for continued strength and hypertrophy gains.

## Core Mechanisms

### 1. Load Progression (Primary)
Increasing the weight lifted is the most direct form of overload:
- Upper body exercises: increase by 1-2.5 kg per session when all prescribed reps are completed
- Lower body exercises: increase by 2.5-5 kg per session when all prescribed reps are completed
- Micro-loading (0.5-1 kg) is valuable for isolation exercises and smaller muscle groups

### 2. Volume Progression
When load cannot be increased, add training volume:
- Add 1 additional set per exercise per week
- Target weekly set volume: 10-20 sets per muscle group for hypertrophy
- Beginners: 10-12 sets/muscle/week; Intermediate: 14-18 sets; Advanced: 16-20+ sets

### 3. Rep Progression
Increase reps within a target range before increasing load:
- Strength: 3-5 rep range, increase weight when 5 reps are achieved
- Hypertrophy: 8-12 rep range, increase weight when 12 reps are achieved for all sets
- Endurance: 15-20 rep range, increase weight when 20 reps are achieved

### 4. Density Progression
Reduce rest periods between sets:
- Strength training: 2-5 minutes rest between compound movements
- Hypertrophy training: 60-90 seconds rest
- Reducing rest by 10-15 seconds per week while maintaining performance

## Programming by Training Age

### Beginners (0-12 months)
- Linear progression: add weight every session
- Full-body training 3x/week
- Focus on compound movements: squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press, row
- RPE 6-7 starting point to master technique

### Intermediate (1-3 years)
- Weekly progression: aim to increase weight or reps weekly
- Upper/Lower or Push/Pull/Legs split
- Periodize intensity and volume across mesocycles (4-6 week blocks)
- Include both compound and isolation work

### Advanced (3+ years)
- Monthly or block periodization
- Undulating periodization: vary rep ranges within the week
- Specialized training blocks for weak points
- Deload every 4-6 weeks (reduce volume by 40-50%)

## Recovery Considerations
- Sleep < 6 hours: reduce training volume by 15-20%, add extra rest day
- Sleep 6-7 hours: maintain current programming
- Sleep 7-9 hours: optimal for progressive overload
- High stress periods: autoregulate with RPE-based training (target RPE 7-8)
